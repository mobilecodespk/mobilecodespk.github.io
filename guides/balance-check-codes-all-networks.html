Balance Check Codes for All Networks in Pakistan

Checking your mobile balance is the most common thing Pakistani subscribers do with a USSD code, and every network has its own code for it. This guide lists the balance check code for Jazz, Zong, Telenor and Ufone in one table, explains what each check costs, and shows you the free alternatives in each operator's official app. Knowing the right code for your network saves time, and knowing the app alternative saves money, because some balance checks carry a small charge while the app check is usually free. Keep this page bookmarked, especially if your family uses more than one network, because the codes are easy to mix up. As always, codes can change, so confirm in the official app if a code stops working.

The four balance check codes

Jazz users dial *111# to check their balance. Zong users dial *222#. Telenor users dial *444#. Ufone users dial *124#. In each case you type the code into the phone dialler and press the call button, and your remaining balance appears on the screen within a few seconds. The pattern is easy to remember once you notice it: each network uses a repeated digit, which is why the codes feel familiar even if you have not dialled them for a while. Write your own network's code on a note near your phone if you keep forgetting it, and you will soon remember it without thinking.

Does checking your balance cost money?

On most Pakistani networks, a balance check by USSD code carries a small charge, often a few rupees, which is deducted from your balance. The exact charge can change, and it is usually mentioned in the reply message or in the operator's terms, so do not be surprised if your balance drops slightly after a check. The free alternative is the official app: Jazz World, My Zong, My Telenor and My Ufone all show your balance on the home screen without any charge, and they also show your active bundles and remaining data. If you check your balance several times a day, using the app instead of the code can save a noticeable amount over a month. Postpaid users generally see their bill and usage in the app rather than a prepaid balance.

When the balance check does not work

If the code returns an error, first check that you have network signal and that your SIM is active. A weak signal is the most common cause of a failed USSD request. Restart your phone and try again after a minute, because the error is often temporary. If the problem continues, try the official app, which uses the internet instead of USSD and will usually work even when USSD fails. If both fail, call your operator's helpline: 111 for Jazz, 310 for Zong, 345 for Telenor and 333 for Ufone. The helpline agent can check your balance and tell you whether there is a network problem in your area.

Good habits for managing your balance

Check your balance before subscribing to a bundle, so the subscription does not fail for lack of credit. Check it again after subscribing, so you can confirm the bundle price was deducted correctly. Keep a small reserve balance for emergencies, because a zero balance means you cannot make calls or send SMS until you top up. If you top up through the app or a banking app, the balance usually updates within a minute, and you can confirm it with a quick check. Finally, teach older family members the code for their network, and show them the app alternative, so they can check their balance without help.

Important: USSD codes and package offers change from time to time. Confirm the current code and price in your operator's official app (Jazz World, My Zong, My Telenor or My Ufone) before you subscribe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the Jazz balance check code?
A: Dial *111# from a Jazz number. Zong is *222#, Telenor is *444# and Ufone is *124#.

Q: Is checking balance free?
A: USSD balance checks usually carry a small charge; checking in the official app is normally free.

Q: Why did my balance check fail?
A: Usually weak signal or a temporary network error. Restart the phone and retry, or check in the official app.
