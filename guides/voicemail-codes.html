Voicemail Codes and How to Use Voicemail

Voicemail is the answering machine built into your mobile network: when you cannot answer a call, the caller can leave a voice message, and you can listen to it later. It is useful when your phone is switched off, when you are in a meeting, or when you are travelling and cannot take calls. Many Pakistani subscribers never set up voicemail, so callers hear only a ringing tone and give up, and the subscriber never knows someone tried to call. This guide explains how voicemail works on Jazz, Zong, Telenor and Ufone, how to set it up, how to listen to your messages, and how to turn it off if you do not want it. Voicemail setup varies by operator, so the official app or helpline is the final authority for your network.

How voicemail works

When someone calls you and you do not answer, or your phone is off or out of coverage, the network can send the call to your voicemail box, where the caller hears a greeting and can record a message. The message is stored by the network, not on your phone, so you can listen to it from any phone by calling your voicemail number and entering your PIN. Voicemail is different from call forwarding, because the call is not sent to another person: it is recorded for you to hear later. Some operators activate voicemail automatically, some need you to activate it, and some charge a small daily or monthly fee, so check the terms for your network before you rely on it.

Setting up voicemail

The simplest way to set up voicemail is through your operator's official app or helpline, because the exact method differs by network. On many networks, voicemail is linked to the conditional call forwarding codes: forwarding unanswered calls to the voicemail number is what sends callers to your mailbox. Your operator's helpline can tell you the voicemail number for your network and activate the service for you. When you first use voicemail, you will be asked to set a PIN and record a greeting, which callers hear before they leave a message. Choose a PIN you will remember, and record a short greeting with your name, so callers know they have reached the right person.

Listening to your messages

When you have a new voicemail, your operator usually sends you an SMS saying a message is waiting. To listen, call your voicemail number, which your operator's helpline or app can give you, and enter your PIN when asked. You can listen to messages, delete them, and save the ones that matter. If you forget your PIN, call the helpline and ask for a reset, after verifying your identity with your CNIC details. Check your voicemail regularly, because old messages may be deleted automatically after a few days, and a full mailbox stops accepting new messages, so callers will hear that your mailbox is full.

Turning voicemail off

If you do not want voicemail, you can turn it off through the official app or by calling the helpline and asking for deactivation. You can also cancel the conditional forwarding that sends calls to voicemail, using the codes ##61# for unanswered calls and ##62# for unreachable, although the exact method depends on how your operator set it up, so confirm with the helpline. After turning it off, ask a friend to call you and let it ring, to confirm that the caller no longer reaches a voicemail greeting. If you change your mind later, the helpline can activate it again. Remember that deactivating voicemail does not delete messages already stored, so listen to them first if they matter.

Important: USSD codes and package offers change from time to time. Confirm the current code and price in your operator's official app (Jazz World, My Zong, My Telenor or My Ufone) before you subscribe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I set up voicemail?
A: Contact your operator through its official app or helpline; the exact method and voicemail number differ by network.

Q: How do I listen to my voicemail messages?
A: Call your voicemail number and enter your PIN. Your operator sends an SMS when a new message arrives.

Q: How do I turn voicemail off?
A: Ask the helpline or use the official app to deactivate it, and confirm by having a friend call you.
